D-FuseSmileNet: Enhancing Spontaneous Smile Recognition

A novel deep learning framework that integrates hand-crafted physiological markers with transformer-based features to distinguish genuine (spontaneous) from fake (posed) smiles, achieving new state-of-the-art results.

Technical Challenge & Innovation

  • Problem: Spontaneous smiles have subtle spatiotemporal differences in muscle activations (e.g., eye crinkling) often missed by generic deep learning models. Traditional multi-task learning (MTL) approaches are complex due to intricate loss-weighting schemes.
  • Innovation (Hadamard Fusion): Proposed a direct Hadamard multiplicative fusion to blend powerful transformer-based visual representations (learned features) with expert-defined D-Marker features (hand-crafted physiological cues).
  • Architecture: Systematically evaluated 15 fusion strategies, confirming that Hadamard fusion achieves optimal performance by enabling meaningful feature interaction while eliminating the need for auxiliary task supervision, which complicates MTL models.
  • Methodology: The approach is fully automated and end-to-end trainable, unlike many high-performing baselines that rely on manual landmark initialization and extensive preprocessing.

Visual and Quantitative Impact

Architecture Diagram (D-FuseSmileNet Pipeline)

100% Accuracy

Achieved perfect classification on the challenging BBC dataset, and 99.7% on MMI, establishing consistent SOTA performance.

+5.0% Gain (BBC)

Outperformed previous multi-task learning methods by significant margins: +5.0% on BBC and +0.8% on UvA-NEMO, validating the superiority of direct feature interaction.

Reduced Complexity

Eliminated the need for auxiliary loss-weighting and hyperparameter tuning required by Multi-Task Learning (MTL) approaches, improving computational efficiency.

LLM-Driven Clinical NER Annotation Pipeline

Designed a scalable, cost-effective annotation pipeline using advanced Large Language Models (LLMs) to automatically label medical entities in low-resource Bengali clinical text, drastically reducing reliance on manual expert effort.

Core Methodology & Innovation

  • Problem: Training clinical Named Entity Recognition (NER) models for low-resource languages (like Bengali) is severely limited by the scarcity and expense of human-labeled data.
  • Innovation (CoT Prompting): Developed a pipeline utilizing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and carefully balanced few-shot demonstrations with models like GPT-4.1 to generate high-fidelity synthetic labels.
  • Efficiency: Demonstrated that annotation quality depends more on the structured reasoning provided by CoT prompt design than on raw LLM capacity (GPT-4.1-mini + CoT outperformed GPT-4.1 ICL).
  • Domain Focus: Conducted detailed corpus analysis of the Bangla-HealthNER dataset to extract domain-specific linguistic features and entity distributions, guiding the prompt design for noisy, code-switched text.

Visual and Quantitative Impact

System Architecture (Annotation Pipeline)

3x F1 Improvement

CoT reasoning almost tripled token-level F1 agreement with human labels (from 0.120 to 0.335) compared to plain prompting.

46.0 Macro F1

The resulting synthetic data trained BanglishBERT to achieve 46.0 F1, closing two-thirds of the gap to fully supervised training (56.13 F1), eliminating manual effort.
